# The Drowned Bell

*A stolen bell, a drowned cult, and a magistrate who wants the whole town to come back changed.*

## Overview

The bell of the [[Chapel of the Tides]] was taken on the night of the spring tide by the [[The Tidecallers|Tidecallers]], who need it for a rite at the [[Bellwether Light]]. When it's rung at high tide, everyone in Greywater who has ever drowned and been brought back walks into the sea — and [[Magistrate Corvin Sloane]], who drowned as a boy, wants to go.

**The clock:** the next spring tide is four days after the party arrives. Ring the bell then, and the town loses a dozen people — including Sloane.

## Scene 3 — The Causeway

The Marsh Road runs a mile out to the lighthouse, and it's only dry at low tide.

!!! read-aloud "The Marsh Road"
    The causeway is a line of old stones through the reeds, slick with weed. Ahead, the lighthouse leans out of the marsh like a drunk against a wall, and somewhere in the water beside you, something large turns over.

- **The tide:** they have two hours before the causeway floods. Every long rest or detour costs one of them.
- **The lurker:** a [[Bog Lurker]] hunts the causeway's edge. The Tidecallers feed it; anyone in an oilskin coat is left alone.

## The Rite

The lower floor of the [[Bellwether Light]] is flooded knee-deep, and the bell hangs from the old lamp chain in the middle of it.

{{ encounter bellwether-rite }}

!!! read-aloud "The flooded floor"
    Lanterns float on the black water, a dozen of them, green as the one over the inn. In the middle of the room the bell hangs from a rusted chain, and a man stands under it up to his waist in the sea, singing a name you almost recognize.

!!! dm "Running the rite"
    [[The Drowned Man]] heals in the water; pulling him onto the stairs, or telling him his name (Marrow — it's on the chapel wall), ends that. Three Tidecallers guard the stairs. If the bell is rung, every drowned-and-returned creature within a mile starts walking — including, back in town, the Magistrate.

## Afterward

- **If the bell comes home**, Sister Amara rings it at dawn and the fleet goes out singing. The chapel's 50 gold, and a standing welcome at the Gull.
- **If Sloane is exposed**, the town turns on him — but he owns half the boats, and the debts on the rest.
